## How to deposit euros with SEPA, step by step

On the web: open **Cash**, pick your **EUR** balance, choose **Bank transfer (SEPA)**, then copy the account details OKX generates for you and start the transfer from your own online banking. Confirm it on the bank side.

In the app: **Transfer → Deposit → Euro → Bank transfer (SEPA)**, making sure EUR is the selected currency, then use the same details in your banking app.

Three things decide whether that transfer arrives cleanly:

**Copy the IBAN exactly.** Do not shorten it, retype it from memory, or "fix" the formatting. OKX matches incoming payments to your account automatically; a mangled IBAN means a delay or manual handling.

**Send from an account in your own name.** The name on your OKX account and the name on the bank account have to match. A transfer from a partner, a friend, or a shared family account will be rejected because the names do not line up.

**Never send from an e-wallet.** Payments from third-party sources, including PayPal, Wise and Airwallex, are not accepted and get returned. The transfer has to originate from your registered bank account.

You also need to have completed identity verification before any of this works. Which payment methods you see depends on the documents you verified with and your country of residence, so a shorter list than the one above is normal.

## How long it takes, and the €100,000 question

SEPA Standard is a banking-hours product: 1–2 business days, and a Friday afternoon transfer may not move until Monday or Tuesday. SEPA Instant is the opposite. If your bank participates and marks the payment as urgent, the money shows up in seconds, at any hour, weekends included. The €100,000 cap applies per transfer on the Instant rail; Standard limits depend on your bank.

The decision rule is simple. If the amount is large and the timing is not critical, send Standard and avoid any bank-side instant limits. If you want to buy today, or you are moving a four-figure sum and do not want to wait out a weekend, use Instant. Check with your bank first about both its SEPA Instant support and its own outgoing fees, because OKX waives its fee but your bank does not have to.

One more thing that surprises people: after a bank transfer, part of your balance can be temporarily locked as an automated risk review. You can see the release date under **Assets → Lock details**.

## Why was my SEPA deposit rejected or delayed?

Check these in order, because they cover most cases:

1. **Name mismatch.** The sending account was not registered in your name.
2. **Third-party payment.** The money came from an e-wallet or a payment provider rather than your bank.
3. **IBAN copied wrong or truncated.** The payment details were missing or incorrect, so it needs manual matching.
4. **Weekend or bank holiday submission.** Processing only started the next business day.
5. **Extra compliance checks.** Your bank applied additional review to a payment going to a financial platform, which it is entitled to do.

Rejected deposits go back to the account they came from automatically. Allow up to 5 business days for the refund to appear, depending on your bank. If your bank shows the transfer as sent but nothing arrives after 2 business days, pull the payment confirmation showing the IBAN you used and take it to OKX support rather than re-sending the money.

## Withdrawing euros: free, but there is a first-time step

SEPA withdrawals are free on OKX's side, with your bank potentially charging its own outgoing fee, and the money usually arrives instantly or within 2 business days. The web flow is **Cash → EUR balance → SEPA → amount → authorisation or SMS code → Withdraw**; in the app it is **Transfer → Withdraw → SEPA**.

The step most guides leave out: before your first withdrawal you have to verify that the receiving bank account belongs to you. OKX does that by having you make a deposit from that same account. Once the deposit is processed, the account is verified and withdrawals unlock. Until then you cannot withdraw, no matter how much balance you hold. So if you plan to move money both ways, make your first SEPA transfer a deposit, not a withdrawal.

## Two limits that only apply in the EEA

**USDT is not tradable here.** If you are moving funds in from another exchange, do not arrive holding USDT and expect to trade it. Since 17 July 2026 a USDT balance in the EEA can only be converted to USDC, one direction. Convert before you transfer, or budget for the swap on arrival.

**Easy Transfer is narrower than SEPA itself.** It is a shortcut that fills in the bank details for you and is open to fully verified users living in the EU plus Norway, Iceland and Liechtenstein. Availability, speed and limits depend on your bank, so treat what your account shows as the source of truth rather than any guide, this one included.

## Who runs the European side, and what to know about it

OKX serves EU customers through OKX Europe Limited in Malta, authorised under MiCA by the Malta Financial Services Authority since 27 January 2025. According to ESMA's interim MiCA register, that licence covers all 30 EEA countries. The company was previously named OKCoin Europe Ltd.

There is also a regulatory record worth knowing. On 1 April 2025, Malta's anti-money-laundering authority, the FIAU, imposed a penalty of €1,054,269 on OKCoin Europe Limited for shortcomings in its AML controls up to April 2023, covering areas including business risk assessment, customer due diligence and transaction monitoring. The FIAU noted at publication that the penalty could still be appealed. OKX publishes monthly proof-of-reserves reports, though these cover the group's entities worldwide rather than OKX Europe alone.

None of that changes how a SEPA transfer works. It does explain why the name-matching and third-party rules are enforced as strictly as they are.
